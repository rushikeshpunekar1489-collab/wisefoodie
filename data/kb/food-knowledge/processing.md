# Understanding Ultra-Processed Foods and the NOVA Scale

The **NOVA Food Classification System**, developed by researchers at the University of São Paulo, categorizes foods based on the nature, extent, and purpose of industrial processing.

### The 4 NOVA Food Groups
1. **Group 1: Unprocessed or Minimally Processed Foods**:
   - Natural parts of plants, animals, or fungi (e.g. fresh cow milk, raw rolled oats, whole unpolished dals, fresh apples, plain whole wheat grain).
   - Minimally processed through cleaning, milling, pasteurization, or freezing without added salt, sugar, or industrial chemicals.
2. **Group 2: Processed Culinary Ingredients**:
   - Substances derived directly from Group 1 foods (e.g. cold-pressed mustard oil, dairy butter, unrefined salt, traditional jaggery). Used in kitchens to cook meals.
3. **Group 3: Processed Foods**:
   - Relatively simple foods manufactured by combining Group 1 and Group 2 ingredients (e.g. artisanal cheese, canned salted vegetables, freshly baked bakery bread with only flour, yeast, water, and salt).
4. **Group 4: Ultra-Processed Foods (UPF)**:
   - Industrial formulations typically manufactured from substances extracted from foods (isolated oils, hydrogenated fats, modified starches, protein isolates, inverted sugars) combined with cosmetic additives (emulsifiers, thickeners, artificial colourings, artificial flavourings, flavor enhancers).
   - Examples: Carbonated soft drinks, sweet sandwich cookies, instant noodles with chemical seasoning packets, extruded neon puffs, reconstituted energy bars.

### The Kitchen Test
A simple rule of thumb: If a food product contains 3 or more ingredients that you could never find in a traditional home kitchen cabinet (such as maltodextrin, INS 471, sodium metabisulphite, or inverted sugar syrup), it is an ultra-processed food.
