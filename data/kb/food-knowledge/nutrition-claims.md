# Decoding Commercial Nutrition Claims

Food brand packaging in India is saturated with regulatory loopholes and marketing claims designed to create a "health halo" around poor nutritional formulations.

### 1. "Zero Cholesterol" / "Cholesterol Free"
- **The Reality**: Frequently advertised on bottles of refined sunflower oil, palm oil, or peanut butter.
- **The Truth**: Cholesterol is synthesized exclusively in animal livers. **No plant-based food or plant oil has ever contained cholesterol.** Stamping "Zero Cholesterol" on cooking oil is like advertising that drinking water is "Fat Free".

### 2. "No Added Sugar"
- **The Reality**: The manufacturer has not dumped crystalline sucrose (white table sugar) into the mixture.
- **The Truth**: The food may be packed with date paste, apple juice concentrate, liquid glucose, or maltodextrin. These rapidly absorb into the bloodstream and trigger virtually identical glycemic and insulin spikes.

### 3. "Trans Fat Free" / "0g Trans Fat"
- **The Reality**: Under FSSAI regulations, a product may claim "0g Trans Fat" if it contains **less than 0.2g of trans fat per serving**.
- **The Truth**: If you eat 3 or 4 servings of a snack fried in partially hydrogenated vegetable fat, you will consume significant industrial trans fat despite the "0g Trans Fat" banner.

### 4. "High Protein" or "Source of Protein"
- **FSSAI Benchmark**: To claim "Source of Protein", at least 10% of energy (or 5g per 100g) must come from protein. For "High Protein", at least 20% of energy (or 10g per 100g) must come from protein.
- Always check the protein-to-sugar ratio: If a bar contains 10g of protein but also 25g of sugar and 380 kcal, it is a candy bar with added protein powder.
