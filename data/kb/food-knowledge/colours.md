# Synthetic Food Colours and Additives

Synthetic food dyes are petroleum-derived coal-tar chemicals added purely for cosmetic visual appeal to make industrial foods look brighter and more appetizing.

### Common Synthetic Food Colours in India
1. **Tartrazine (INS 102)**: Synthetic bright yellow dye used in aerated drinks, yellow candies, extruded snacks, and instant soups.
2. **Sunset Yellow FCF (INS 110)**: Bright orange dye frequently used in orange soft drinks, mango flavoured drinks, and confectionery.
3. **Ponceau 4R (INS 124) & Allura Red (INS 129)**: Bright synthetic red dyes.
4. **Brilliant Blue FCF (INS 133)**: Synthetic blue dye.
5. **Caramel Colour (INS 150d / Class IV Caramel)**: Produced by heating carbohydrates with ammonia and sulfite compounds; used to darken colas, ready curries, and brown breads.

### The Southampton Additives Warning
In the European Union and the UK, foods containing synthetic azo dyes (INS 102, 110, 122, 124, 129) must carry a mandatory warning: *"May have an adverse effect on activity and attention in children."* In India, these dyes remain legally permitted in packaged foods under specified dosage limits.

### Safe Natural Alternatives
Look for packaged foods coloured with natural plant pigments:
- **Curcumin / Turmeric** (INS 100)
- **Beetroot Red / Betanin** (INS 162)
- **Paprika Oleoresin / Capsanthin** (INS 160c)
- **Chlorophyll** (INS 140)
