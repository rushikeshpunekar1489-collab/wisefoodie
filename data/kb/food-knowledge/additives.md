# Decoding INS Numbers and Food Additives

The **International Numbering System (INS)** is a globally standardized numeric code system adopted by FSSAI in India to identify permitted food additives. In Europe, these same numbers are prefixed with an "E" (e.g. INS 330 = E330).

### Common Functional Classes of Additives
1. **Preservatives (INS 200–299)**: Inhibit microbial growth and extend shelf life (e.g., Potassium Sorbate INS 202, Sodium Benzoate INS 211).
2. **Antioxidants & Acidity Regulators (INS 300–399)**:
   - *INS 300*: Ascorbic Acid (Vitamin C) — benign antioxidant.
   - *INS 330*: Citric Acid — natural fruit acid used for tart flavour.
   - *INS 322*: Lecithin — natural emulsifier extracted from soy or sunflower seeds.
3. **Thickeners, Stabilizers & Emulsifiers (INS 400–499)**:
   - *INS 471*: Mono- and diglycerides of fatty acids — keeps fats and water blended in ice creams and bakery items.
   - *INS 412*: Guar Gum — natural legume seed gum providing texture.
   - *INS 407*: Carrageenan — seaweed extract; frequent intestinal irritant in sensitive individuals.
4. **Flavour Enhancers (INS 600–699)**:
   - *INS 621*: Monosodium Glutamate (MSG).
   - *INS 635*: Disodium 5'-ribonucleotides — ultra-potent umami booster used in snacks and soups.

### The WiseFoodies Processing Rule
Not all additives are harmful; however, foods containing **5 or more distinct synthetic industrial additives** (emulsifiers, thickeners, artificial colourings, flavour enhancers) represent ultra-processed formulations and receive a dedicated penalty in our Food Benefit Score (FBS).
