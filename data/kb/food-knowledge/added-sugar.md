# The Many Aliases of Added Sugar

Food manufacturers in India regularly divide sugar across multiple separate ingredients so that table sugar does not appear as the single top ingredient.

### Common Sugar Synonyms on Indian Labels
1. **Invert Sugar / Invert Syrup**: A split glucose-fructose syrup used in biscuits and cakes to maintain moisture and softness.
2. **Liquid Glucose**: Starch hydrolysate widely used in candies, breakfast spreads, chocolates, and jams.
3. **Maltodextrin**: A white enzymatic powder used as a bulking agent. It tastes mildly sweet but has a glycemic index (GI) between 105 and 135 — higher than pure sucrose table sugar.
4. **High Fructose Corn Syrup (HFCS)**: Concentrated liquid fructose sweetener.
5. **Fruit Juice Concentrate**: Frequently marketed as "real fruit sweetness", yet behaves biochemically like liquid refined sugar.
6. **Dextrose, Maltose, Sucrose, Caramel**: Any ingredient ending in "-ose" indicates simple sugar.

### Daily Sugar Thresholds
- **ICMR-NIN & WHO Guideline**: Recommended maximum added sugar intake is **under 25 grams per day** (about 5-6 teaspoons) for a healthy adult.
- A single bottle of soda or a packet of sweet sandwich biscuits can deliver 30g to 45g of added sugar in a single sitting, immediately exhausting your daily allowance.
